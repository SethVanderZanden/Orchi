# Reduce Interaction Cost

## Trace the real task

1. Define the user's goal, starting context, completion signal, and recovery path.
2. Walk the complete flow and record decisions, inputs, navigation, pointer or touch travel, waits, context switches, and repeated work.
3. Separate necessary effort from effort created by the interface.
4. Identify the highest-cost moments rather than optimizing raw click count.

## Reduce cognitive effort

- Use recognizable language, sensible defaults, clear grouping, and visible status.
- Replace recall with recognition.
- Defer irrelevant choices until they matter.
- Explain unfamiliar consequences close to the decision.
- Preserve context across steps and return visits.

## Reduce physical effort

- Enlarge or reposition frequent targets appropriately for the input method.
- Keep related controls near the content they affect.
- Support keyboard navigation, paste, autofill, bulk actions, and efficient repetition.
- Avoid precision-dependent gestures and long pointer travel.
- Prevent accidental activation of adjacent or destructive controls.

## Reduce time effort

- Remove redundant steps, fields, confirmations, and reloads.
- Reuse known information with permission.
- Show progress and immediate feedback.
- Use optimistic updates only when failure is safely reversible and communicated.
- Allow users to resume long or interrupted tasks.

## Preserve necessary friction

- Keep deliberate checkpoints for irreversible, costly, legal, security-sensitive, or high-impact actions.
- Prefer undo or recovery when it safely replaces confirmation.
- Do not remove context merely to reduce steps.
- Measure task success, errors, and confidence alongside completion time.

## Avoid these failures

- Do not optimize only for the shortest happy path.
- Do not treat every click as equal.
- Do not automate choices users must understand or control.
- Do not make frequent actions faster by making rare mistakes catastrophic.
- Do not claim an improvement without tracing the revised flow.

## Produce the result

Provide:

1. A step-by-step cost map.
2. The largest cognitive, physical, and time costs.
3. A revised flow with removed, combined, automated, or deferred effort.
4. Necessary safeguards and recovery paths.
5. Measures for validating task success and effort.
